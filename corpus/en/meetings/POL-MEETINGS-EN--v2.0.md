# Meetings Policy

## 1. Purpose
The Company establishes this policy to ensure that all internal and external gatherings are conducted with maximum efficiency, respect for employee time, and clear accountability. In a fast-paced corporate environment, structured discussions are vital for maintaining productivity and driving strategic objectives forward. Poorly managed gatherings can drain operational energy and delay project delivery. By setting clear guidelines on scheduling, preparation, execution, and follow-up, the Company aims to eliminate unnecessary time waste and foster a culture of purposeful communication. This document outlines the operational framework that every employee must follow when organizing, attending, or managing professional discussions across all departments and teams.

## 2. Scope
This policy applies universally to all full-time employees, part-time staff, contractors, and management personnel operating within the Company, regardless of their physical location or departmental alignment. Whether teams are collaborating inside physical corporate offices or participating remotely via digital platforms, these rules govern their scheduling habits and meeting behaviors. The framework covers all forms of professional collaboration, including routine team check-ins, departmental reviews, cross-functional project syncs, and discussions involving external stakeholders such as clients, vendors, and partners. Adherence to these standards is mandatory for all personnel to ensure operational consistency and professional courtesy throughout the organization.

## 3. Policy
Effective collaboration requires strict adherence to timing, preparation, and scheduling boundaries within the Company. To achieve this, several operational rules govern every gathering. 

First, preparation is mandatory. The person organizing the discussion must share the meeting agenda at least 24 hours before the meeting begins. This ensures that all participants have adequate time to review topics, prepare necessary materials, and contribute meaningfully. Similarly, invitees must respond promptly by accepting, declining, or marking an invitation as tentative within 24 hours of receiving the calendar invite, allowing organizers to gauge attendance accurately.

Second, time management is critical for operational flow. Internal gatherings should not exceed 60 minutes in duration. If a complex topic requires a longer session, organizers must incorporate a structured break to maintain attendee focus and energy. Furthermore, to protect employee well-being and personal time heading into the weekend, no internal meetings may be scheduled after 15:00 on Thursdays. 

Third, approval protocols apply to specific types of gatherings. Any discussions involving external participants or more than 10 attendees require formal approval from the department head before invitations can be officially confirmed and sent out. This prevents resource strain and ensures leadership oversight on large-scale commitments.

Fourth, space utilization must be efficient. Meeting rooms are booked exclusively through the shared room calendar. To prevent wasted space, any room bookings not used within 10 minutes of the start time are automatically released for other teams to utilize.

Finally, post-discussion accountability is essential. Meeting minutes and action items must be shared within 24 hours after the meeting concludes, ensuring that assigned tasks are clearly documented and tracked for successful execution.

## 4. Responsibilities
Maintaining these standards is a shared duty across different roles within the Company. 

Organizers bear the primary responsibility for the success of any gathering. They must define clear objectives, distribute the agenda at least 24 hours before the meeting, secure necessary department head approvals for large or external sessions, and ensure that meeting minutes and action items are shared within 24 hours after the meeting ends. Organizers must also respect time boundaries, keeping internal sessions under 60 minutes and avoiding scheduling anything after 15:00 on Thursdays.

Invitees also carry specific duties. They must review incoming invites and accept, decline, or mark them as tentative within 24 hours. During the session, participants are expected to engage constructively and arrive prepared based on the distributed agenda. 

Department heads are responsible for reviewing and authorizing requests for gatherings that include external participants or exceed 10 attendees, ensuring business necessity before confirmation. 

Facilities and administrative teams maintain the shared room calendar system, while all employees utilizing physical rooms are responsible for respecting the 10-minute start rule so that unused spaces are freed up promptly.

## 5. Exceptions
While this policy applies broadly across the organization, rare operational edge cases may arise where strict adherence to specific rules is impractical. For example, critical emergency situations, major system outages, or urgent crisis management involving senior leadership may require immediate discussions that bypass the standard requirement to share an agenda at least 24 hours before the meeting. Similarly, urgent client escalations might necessitate scheduling internal discussions after 15:00 on Thursdays, or holding sessions that exceed 60 minutes without standard breaks due to binding external deadlines. 

Any deviation from these standard rules, such as bypassing department head approvals for large gatherings or extending timelines beyond the stipulated windows, requires documented justification and prior authorization from senior management. Such exemptions are reviewed on a case-by-case basis to ensure they do not become routine practices, thereby preserving the integrity of the Company’s operational standards.
