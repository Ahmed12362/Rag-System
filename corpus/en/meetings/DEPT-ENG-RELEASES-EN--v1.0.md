# Engineering Release and Meeting Rules

## 1. Purpose
This document establishes core operational standards for the Engineering department to maintain system stability and respect team schedules. The Company enforces these guidelines to optimize daily workflows, prevent deployment-related emergencies before weekends, and ensure that technical staff can focus on deep work without late-day interruptions. By adhering to these protocols, the organization fosters a reliable engineering culture that prioritizes product quality, proactive planning, and sustainable work habits across all development teams.

## 2. Applies to
This policy applies exclusively to the Engineering department. Every software engineer, QA specialist, DevOps professional, and technical manager within this specific organizational unit must follow these operational rules. No other business units or corporate divisions are covered by this particular mandate, ensuring that engineering-specific workflows remain targeted and effective.

## 3. Department rule
The department operates under two strict scheduling prohibitions designed to protect deployment integrity and employee time. First, production releases are not deployed on Thursdays. Engineers must plan their release cycles, staging validations, and deployment windows earlier in the week to accommodate potential rollbacks or debugging sessions during standard working hours. Second, no internal meetings may be scheduled after 15:00 on Thursdays. This restriction includes all syncs, retrospectives, planning sessions, and cross-functional reviews, providing staff with uninterrupted afternoon time to wrap up ongoing tasks, document weekly progress, and transition smoothly toward the weekend.

## 4. Rationale
The reasoning behind these procedural constraints is rooted in risk mitigation and productivity preservation. Launching code changes late in the work week drastically increases the likelihood of unresolved production incidents occurring outside active support hours, placing an undue burden on on-call personnel. Furthermore, prohibiting late afternoon gatherings on Thursdays prevents fatigue and allows technical contributors to conclude their weekly objectives calmly. When teams avoid last-minute deployments and late day discussions, overall system uptime improves and staff morale remains high.

## 5. Escalation
If scheduling conflicts or urgent business demands arise that challenge these operational boundaries, employees must consult their direct engineering managers immediately. Managers are responsible for reviewing exceptional circumstances, coordinating with relevant stakeholders, and rescheduling prohibited meetings or deployment tasks to appropriate days and times. Bypassing these procedures without prior managerial approval undermines the stability standards set by the Company and may trigger a formal review of the department's release management practices.
