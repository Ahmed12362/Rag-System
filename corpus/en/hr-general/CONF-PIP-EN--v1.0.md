# Performance Improvement Plans
## 1. Purpose
The primary objective of this document is to establish a clear, structured framework that the Company uses to address and support employees who are experiencing performance challenges. Human Resources and management utilize this internal guideline to ensure fairness, transparency, and consistency across all departments. By setting clear expectations and offering structured support, the Company aims to help team members overcome obstacles, bridge competency gaps, and successfully meet the performance standards required for their roles. This procedure outlines the methodology for identifying performance deficiencies, designing targeted corrective actions, monitoring progress, and determining appropriate outcomes. Managers and HR professionals must follow this process meticulously to protect employee rights while maintaining high organizational productivity and accountability standards.

## 2. Who this applies to
This internal procedure strictly applies to all management personnel and Human Resources staff operating within the Company. It governs how leaders handle performance management scenarios for full-time and part-time personnel whose output or behavioral metrics fall below expected benchmarks. Management must consult with HR before initiating any formal corrective measures to ensure compliance with internal policies. While the employee experiencing performance challenges is the central focus of the process, direct supervisors and HR representatives bear the operational responsibility of drafting, administering, and reviewing the corrective plan. This document is classified as strictly confidential and is restricted solely to internal HR and management use, prohibiting any distribution outside these authorized groups.

## 3. Steps
Administering a performance correction requires a careful, multi-phased approach led by management and HR. The process begins when a supervisor identifies a persistent shortfall in an employee's output or execution of responsibilities. 
First, the manager schedules a private preliminary discussion with the employee to address the specific performance concerns transparently, gather context, and discuss potential obstacles. 
Second, in collaboration with HR, the manager drafts a formal improvement strategy outlining precise, measurable objectives, necessary resources, and expected milestones. 
Third, the manager officially launches the formal process by presenting the document to the employee, explaining every requirement, and answering any procedural questions. 
Fourth, throughout the duration of the process, the supervisor conducts regular progress reviews and provides constructive feedback to guide the employee toward success. 
Fifth, at the conclusion of the corrective period, management and HR evaluate the final results against the initial benchmarks to determine if the employee has successfully met the standards, needs further action, or requires alternative administrative measures.

## 4. Timelines
Managing performance effectively requires structured timeframes to ensure that individuals have a fair opportunity to demonstrate improvement while keeping operations efficient. Specifically, a performance improvement plan lasts 60 days. Within this total duration, supervisors and employees must hold formal check-ins every 2 weeks to assess ongoing progress, review completed tasks, and adjust day-to-day priorities if necessary. These periodic reviews ensure that there are no surprises at the final evaluation and give the employee continuous feedback. If extenuating circumstances arise, such as extended approved leave, HR may review the timeline adjustments on a case-by-case basis, provided such exceptions align with internal governance rules.

## 5. Contacts
For any questions, guidance, or support regarding the administration of performance management procedures within the Company, authorized personnel must reach out exclusively to the designated internal channels. Management should direct all inquiries concerning document interpretation, drafting corrective objectives, or scheduling review meetings to the central Human Resources department. HR representatives are available to assist supervisors in navigating edge cases, maintaining documentation confidentiality, and ensuring that all procedural steps are executed in accordance with Company standards. Direct communication channels and internal messaging platforms should be utilized for all coordination between managers and HR staff.
