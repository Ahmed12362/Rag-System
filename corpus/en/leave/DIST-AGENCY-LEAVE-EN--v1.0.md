# Leave Arrangements for Agency Staff

## 1. Purpose
The primary objective of this internal document is to clarify the administrative framework regarding time off for external personnel operating within the workplace. Maintaining operational transparency and ensuring alignment with external vendor agreements are critical priorities. Leadership recognizes that temporary personnel and contractors require adequate time away from their daily tasks to maintain well-being and productivity. However, because these individuals are engaged through distinct contractual channels, their entitlements differ significantly from those of standard personnel. This policy ensures that internal managers, external suppliers, and the contracted individuals themselves understand the governance surrounding time away from duties. By establishing these guidelines, the Company prevents confusion and ensures smooth collaboration across all operational departments where external talent is deployed.

## 2. Scope
This document applies exclusively to agency staff supplied by external manpower vendors. It is important to note that this policy does not change the rules for regular Company employees, who remain governed by standard internal employment regulations and distinct benefit structures. The guidelines detailed herein cover all departments, business units, and operational sites utilizing external vendor personnel. Whether an individual is assigned to a temporary project, seasonal support, or specialized technical roles through an external agency, their time-off entitlements are strictly bound by the parameters outlined in this policy. Managers overseeing external talent must familiarize themselves with these distinctions to ensure proper operational planning and compliance with external contracts.

## 3. Policy
The core policy governing temporary talent centers on contractual entitlements provided directly through third-party suppliers. Specifically, agency staff supplied by external manpower vendors receive 14 days of leave per year under their agency contract, not under Company policy. This distinction is fundamental to how temporary personnel manage their schedules. When an individual contracted through an external manpower vendor wishes to take time away, they must coordinate directly with their supplying agency and supervising manager to ensure coverage. The standard internal leave accruals, holiday schedules, and time-off frameworks that apply to permanent staff do not extend to external agency workers. Any requests exceeding the stipulated agency contract provisions must be resolved between the individual and their external employer, as the Company does not finance, accrue, or directly administer these specific time-off allotments.

## 4. Responsibilities
Managing the presence and scheduling of external personnel requires active cooperation between internal supervisors, external vendors, and the individual workers. Department managers who utilize external talent are responsible for tracking daily attendance, ensuring operational continuity, and communicating project deadlines effectively. Managers must verify that any time-away requests align with operational demands and are formally logged through the appropriate channels agreed upon with the external supplier. Meanwhile, external manpower vendors hold the primary duty of managing the contracts, compensation, and specific benefit provisions of their supplied personnel, ensuring that the allocated days are administered correctly according to their agreements. Finally, the individual agency workers are responsible for providing adequate notice to their internal supervisors before utilizing their time away, thereby minimizing any disruption to ongoing business activities and maintaining professional accountability.

## 5. Exceptions
Given the structured nature of external vendor agreements, deviations from the standard arrangements are rarely permitted. Any scenario involving extended absences, special operational requirements, or unique scheduling adjustments must be reviewed on a case-by-case basis by the relevant department head in consultation with the external manpower vendor. Under no circumstances can internal management unilaterally alter or increase the time-off provisions stipulated in the external contract. If an external worker requires additional time beyond their contractual allowance, such arrangements must be negotiated directly between the worker and their supplying agency, often resulting in unpaid absence from the workplace. The Company maintains a strict boundary regarding these vendor-managed contracts to ensure fairness, legal compliance, and consistency across all external engagements.

## 6. Related documents
To ensure a comprehensive understanding of workforce management and vendor relations, this document should be reviewed in conjunction with other relevant operational guidelines. These include general supplier management frameworks, onboarding guides for external contractors, and corporate compliance standards regarding workplace conduct. While this policy specifically addresses time away for external personnel, broader workplace safety and security guidelines apply equally to all individuals performing duties at any Company facility. Supervisors and administrators should regularly consult internal HR repositories to access the most up-to-date reference materials concerning contractor oversight, vendor management protocols, and operational workflows.
