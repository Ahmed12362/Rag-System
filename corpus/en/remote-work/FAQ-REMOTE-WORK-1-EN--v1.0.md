# Remote Work FAQ - Part 1

## 1. Which departments are permitted to participate in the remote work program?
The Company offers remote work opportunities strictly to specific organizational units. Employees belonging to the Engineering, IT, Marketing, Data and Analytics, Product, User Experience, Mobile, Infrastructure and Information Security departments are eligible to apply. Conversely, personnel assigned to any other department are required to perform their duties exclusively on-site at the Company premises. Managers across all eligible units must ensure operational alignment when team members request this arrangement, ensuring that coverage remains robust and customer or project deliverables are not impacted by off-site days.

## 2. How many days each week can staff members work away from the office?
Qualified personnel have the opportunity to request up to 2 days per week for working outside the physical office space. This limit is designed to balance the flexibility of telecommuting with the undeniable benefits of in-person collaboration, team brainstorming, and interpersonal connectivity. Staff members cannot accumulate or carry forward unused remote days from one period to another. It is the responsibility of each employee to plan their weekly schedule carefully, ensuring that critical collaborative sessions and major departmental meetings align with their on-site presence days.

## 3. Is supervisory consent required before starting an off-site arrangement?
Yes, obtaining the explicit consent of the direct manager is a mandatory prerequisite for any staff member wishing to telecommute. The supervisor evaluates the request based on operational needs, individual performance history, and the nature of the tasks assigned to the employee. Without this formal approval, staff members must remain at their designated office location. If a manager identifies a decline in productivity or a disruption in team communication, they hold the authority to pause or revoke the telecommuting privilege, requiring the employee to return to the office full-time until further review.

## 4. Can staff from ineligible departments request exceptions to the on-site rule?
Personnel working in departments outside the approved list must adhere to the full-time on-site policy established by the Company. Departments such as those not explicitly mentioned in the approved roster cannot participate in remote arrangements. This distinction ensures fairness and operational consistency across diverse business units, as certain roles inherently require physical presence for equipment handling, facility management, or direct administrative oversight. Supervisors in non-eligible areas cannot grant individual waivers, and exceptions are not processed through human resources for staff in restricted job functions.

## 5. What factors should a supervisor consider when evaluating a remote schedule?
When reviewing a request for up to 2 days per week of off-site work, direct managers must carefully weigh several operational aspects. Supervisors should look at whether the applicant's daily responsibilities can be executed effectively without face-to-face supervision, how independent the worker is, and whether the team's overall productivity will be sustained. Edge cases, such as overlapping remote schedules within the same team leading to understaffed office days, must also be managed proactively by the supervisor to maintain balanced daily coverage and seamless workflow execution.

## 6. How does the arrangement function for multidisciplinary or cross-departmental teams?
Employees who collaborate closely with colleagues across various departments must coordinate their off-site schedule with their direct manager while keeping cross-functional dependencies in mind. Even if an employee belongs to an eligible unit such as Engineering, IT, Marketing, Data and Analytics, Product, User Experience, Mobile, Infrastructure and Information Security, their chosen days away from the desk must not hinder joint projects with colleagues from on-site units or other divisions. Clear communication channels and adherence to shared calendar tools remain vital responsibilities for every participant in the remote work program.
