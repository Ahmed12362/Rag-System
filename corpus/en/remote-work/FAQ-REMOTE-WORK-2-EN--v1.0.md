# Remote Work FAQ - Part 2

## 1. How far in advance do I need to submit my request to work remotely?
When planning to work away from the physical office, staff members are required to submit their schedule through the internal portal beforehand. Specifically, remote days must be requested in the portal at least 2 working days in advance. This advance notice allows management and team leads to coordinate workloads, plan collaborative sessions, and ensure adequate coverage for all ongoing projects. Submitting requests too late may lead to scheduling conflicts and potential rejection of the application by supervisors. Employees should always check their calendars to ensure compliance with this timeline, especially when planning around critical business deliverables or urgent client meetings. In edge cases where unforeseen circumstances arise on short notice, standard office attendance policies typically apply unless emergency management grants an exception. 

## 2. What are the mandatory online availability hours on remote work days?
Working away from the physical premises of the Company comes with the responsibility of maintaining clear lines of communication with colleagues and leadership. On remote days employees must be online and reachable during core hours from 10:00 to 15:00. During this specific window, staff should monitor their messaging applications, respond promptly to emails, and attend any scheduled virtual meetings. This requirement guarantees smooth team operations and prevents bottlenecks in project workflows. Even if a worker prefers flexible hours for the remainder of their day, this central block of time is non-negotiable and requires active engagement. Failure to remain accessible during these specified times may result in a review of remote work privileges.

## 3. How is my daily attendance tracked when I am working remotely?
Monitoring time and attendance remains a critical compliance measure for the organization, regardless of physical location. Attendance is recorded by the office badge reader or, on approved remote days, by checking in through the HR mobile app. Using the mobile application correctly ensures that staff members are properly marked as present and working. It is the responsibility of each individual to verify that their check-in is successfully registered in the system at the start of their shift. Neglecting to log attendance via the correct channel can lead to discrepancies in payroll records and perceived absenteeism. Team members should familiarize themselves with the application interface well in advance to avoid technical hurdles on their scheduled off-site days.

## 4. What steps should I take if the HR mobile app fails to record my check-in?
Technical glitches can occasionally happen with any digital tool, including the attendance platform. If an employee encounters a technical failure while attempting to log their presence, they must immediately notify their direct supervisor and the human resources department. Providing a timestamped screenshot of the error message can help resolve attendance discrepancies quickly. To prevent disciplinary flags, staff should address these issues on the same day they occur rather than waiting until the end of the week. HR will then manually verify the status based on communication logs and online activity during the mandatory availability window, ensuring that the employee is not unfairly penalized for a system outage.

## 5. Can I combine my remote work request with other scheduling policies?
Integrating remote days with other organizational policies, such as standard leave or flexible hours, requires careful planning and coordination. Staff must still adhere strictly to the submission timeline for their off-site days, regardless of any other concurrent schedule adjustments. For example, if an employee intends to work remotely adjacent to a holiday or approved time off, the portal request must still be completed in a timely manner. Managers evaluate these combined requests on a case-by-case basis to ensure that team productivity and operational continuity remain unaffected. Employees should consult internal guidelines or speak with HR if they are unsure how remote policies interact with other workplace regulations.

## 6. What happens if my remote work request is submitted past the deadline?
Submitting paperwork after the designated cutoff period creates administrative challenges for supervisors who rely on accurate roster planning. If an application is filed late through the digital system, the automated workflow or the manager may decline the request. In such scenarios, the employee is expected to report to the physical office as usual to avoid unauthorized absence records. Managers possess the discretion to make rare exceptions for urgent operational needs, but relying on exceptions is strongly discouraged. Staff are advised to set personal calendar reminders to ensure all off-site applications are submitted well ahead of the required timeframe.
