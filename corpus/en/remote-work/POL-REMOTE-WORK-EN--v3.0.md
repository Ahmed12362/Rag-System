# Remote Work Policy (Draft)

## 1. Purpose
This document serves as version 3.0 of a draft proposal for the Company. Please note that this text is currently a draft prepared strictly for consultation purposes and is not yet in force or applicable across the organization. Should the proposals detailed herein ultimately receive official approval, the planned implementation date for these guidelines would be 2027-01-01. The primary objective of establishing these draft guidelines is to evaluate flexible working arrangements while maintaining operational excellence and productivity. The Company continually reviews its internal frameworks to support staff well-being, improve work-life balance, and adapt to modern professional environments. This draft outlines the foundational concepts under review, detailing how staff might balance office-based presence with flexible arrangements. Feedback from internal stakeholders during the consultation phase will help shape the final version of the guidelines before any formal adoption occurs. Managers and team members are encouraged to review these proposals thoughtfully and provide constructive input to the human resources department regarding feasibility, potential challenges, and department-specific impacts.

## 2. Scope
The scope of this proposal specifically addresses where and how different teams within the organization perform their daily tasks. Under the proposed framework, eligible employees would be allowed to work remotely up to 3 days per week. This threshold is designed to ensure that staff maintain sufficient face-to-face collaboration and team cohesion while enjoying the benefits of flexibility. Regarding departmental applicability, the option for remote arrangements is currently available exclusively within a defined subset of divisions across the Company. Specifically, these departments include Engineering, IT, Marketing, Data and Analytics, Product, User Experience, Mobile, Infrastructure, and Information Security. Conversely, all other departments and operational units within the organization are required to work on-site on a full-time basis due to the nature of their duties, specialized equipment requirements, or physical presence necessities. Edge cases, such as cross-departmental projects or transitional roles, will be evaluated on a case-by-case basis during the review period to determine appropriate classification under this scope.

## 3. Policy
The operational policy detailed in this draft governs the structure of flexible arrangements for qualifying personnel. Eligible individuals who belong to the approved departments may split their working schedule between home and the office, provided they do not exceed the maximum remote allowance. Specifically, eligible employees would be allowed to work remotely up to 3 days per week, leaving the remaining working days to be fulfilled from a designated corporate office location. The Company emphasizes that working away from the office is a privilege and a shared responsibility, requiring consistent communication, adherence to security protocols, and reliable availability during standard operating hours. Examples of successful implementation include scheduling focused tasks such as coding, data analysis, content creation, or system architecture design on remote days, while reserving collaborative brainstorming, strategic planning, and hardware setups for on-site days. If an employee's role falls outside the approved divisions—meaning they belong to departments other than Engineering, IT, Marketing, Data and Analytics, Product, User Experience, Mobile, Infrastructure, and Information Security—they must continue to work on-site. Any unauthorized or excessive remote days will be addressed by management to ensure fairness and operational continuity across all teams.

## 4. Responsibilities
Successful execution of these proposed guidelines relies on clear duties assigned to both management and staff members throughout the organization. Department heads and direct supervisors within Engineering, IT, Marketing, Data and Analytics, Product, User Experience, Mobile, Infrastructure, and Information Security are responsible for coordinating schedules, monitoring output, and ensuring that the designated remote limit is strictly respected. Specifically, leadership must ensure that team members do not exceed the threshold where eligible employees would be allowed to work remotely up to 3 days per week. Furthermore, supervisors must guarantee that communication channels remain open and that on-site coverage is adequate every single day. Employees utilizing flexible arrangements bear the responsibility of maintaining a secure, distraction-free home workspace, meeting all performance metrics, and attending mandatory in-person meetings regardless of their planned remote schedule. In departments where staff must work on-site, managers are responsible for fostering an inclusive culture and ensuring equitable treatment compared to teams with flexible options. Edge cases, such as technical failures at home or sudden operational demands requiring immediate physical presence, require staff to commute to the office promptly.

## 5. Exceptions
While the proposed framework establishes clear boundaries for flexible schedules, certain unique situations may warrant special consideration by the Company. Exceptions to the general department eligibility rules—such as temporary medical accommodations or special project assignments—will be reviewed individually by human resources and senior management. However, the core limitation regarding the frequency of flexible days remains a central standard under review. Even when exceptions are granted for specific personal or operational reasons, the overarching principle that eligible employees would be allowed to work remotely up to 3 days per week serves as a baseline cap to prevent full-time absence from the physical office. Personnel working in departments other than Engineering, IT, Marketing, Data and Analytics, Product, User Experience, Mobile, Infrastructure, and Information Security are generally not considered for routine remote arrangements unless a formal, approved exception applies to their unique role. All exception requests must be submitted in writing and are subject to revocation if operational needs change or if the arrangement negatively impacts team productivity.

## 6. Related documents
To ensure a comprehensive understanding of workplace standards, this draft should be read in conjunction with other internal guidelines and corporate governance frameworks maintained by the Company. Related reference materials include the internal code of conduct, data privacy guidelines, information security protocols, and standard operating procedures for equipment usage. Although this text represents a draft proposal, these complementary documents remain fully active and applicable to all personnel regardless of their department or working location. As the organization prepares for the potential transition associated with the target effective date of 2027-01-01, additional supporting documentation and frequently asked questions will be published to assist staff and management. Stakeholders are advised to consult the human resources portal regularly for the most up-to-date versions of all related corporate policies and governance materials.
