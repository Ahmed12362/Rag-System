# Public Holidays FAQ - Part 2

## 1. How does the Company calculate leave durations?
When planning time off, employees need to understand how days are tallied. The Company counts leave in working days, which span from Sunday to Thursday. Weekends, specifically Friday and Saturday, as well as any official public holidays, are excluded from this tally. This means that if an employee takes time off that bridges a weekend or an official public holiday, those specific non-working days will not be deducted from their overall leave balance. Team members and human resources personnel must ensure that time-off requests accurately reflect only the active working days within the requested period to prevent miscalculations in the system. For instance, if someone requests a break that includes a Friday, Saturday, or an official public holiday, those dates remain intact in their accrued balance, ensuring fair treatment and accurate tracking across all departments.

## 2. What happens when leave overlaps with official public holidays?
Official public holidays play a crucial role in how time off is structured. When an official public holiday occurs during an employee's approved leave period, that specific date is not deducted from any leave balance. This ensures that staff members do not lose their holiday entitlements simply because they happened to be away on authorized leave. Managers and human resources administrators are responsible for reviewing leave calendars to verify that public holidays are properly excluded from the total deduction. In edge cases where a holiday falls right in the middle of a multi-week absence, the system automatically bypasses that date, ensuring the employee's balance is preserved. This policy guarantees that everyone receives the full benefit of official public holidays regardless of their individual scheduling or time-off status.

## 3. When can employees expect their monthly compensation from the Company?
Financial predictability is a key priority for all staff members. Salaries are paid on the 25th day of each month, providing a reliable schedule for personal budgeting and financial planning. The payroll department is responsible for processing and disbursing these payments promptly so that employees receive their funds on time. Managers and supervisors should also be aware of this timeline when coordinating project deadlines or financial paperwork with the finance team. This consistent schedule helps maintain high morale and ensures that financial operations within the organization run smoothly and predictably month after month.

## 4. How are salary payment dates adjusted if the 25th falls on a weekend?
Calendars can sometimes present scheduling conflicts with standard payment dates. When the 25th day of the month falls on a weekend, payment is made on the previous working day. This ensures that employees do not experience delays in receiving their monthly compensation due to non-working days. The payroll team must monitor the calendar proactively to identify months where the 25th lands on a Friday or Saturday, executing the transfer earlier to accommodate the schedule shift. For example, if the 25th is a Saturday, the disbursement will take place on the preceding Thursday, ensuring staff have access to their funds before the weekend begins.

## 5. What is the procedure if the payment date coincides with an official holiday?
Official public holidays can occasionally overlap with regular payroll schedules. When the 25th day of the month falls on a holiday, payment is made on the previous working day. The finance department bears the responsibility of cross-referencing the official holiday calendar with the monthly payroll timeline to catch these occurrences well in advance. This proactive approach prevents any disruption in cash flow for the workforce. In edge cases where a public holiday immediately follows a weekend, the payroll team must carefully calculate the correct preceding working day to execute the transfer without error, ensuring compliance with the Company standards.

## 6. Who is responsible for managing payroll timing and leave deductions?
Clear accountability ensures that all organizational policies are applied fairly and accurately. Human resources and payroll departments share the primary responsibility for overseeing leave counts and salary disbursements across the Company. Human resources personnel must ensure that leave is counted in working days, meaning Sunday to Thursday, while confirming that Friday, Saturday, and official public holidays are not deducted from any leave balance. Meanwhile, the payroll team must guarantee that salaries are paid on the 25th day of each month, and adjust accordingly when it falls on a weekend or holiday, ensuring payment is made on the previous working day. Employees also hold a shared responsibility to submit their time-off requests accurately, keeping these operational guidelines in mind to facilitate smooth processing by the administrative teams.
