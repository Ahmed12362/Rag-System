# Engineering On-Call Policy

## 1. Purpose
The primary objective of this document is to establish a clear framework for managing after-hours technical emergencies at the Company. To maintain high availability and reliability of our production systems, the organization requires a reliable mechanism for handling urgent issues that arise outside standard operating hours. This policy ensures that team members understand their obligations when participating in support schedules, defining expectations for rapid response and systematic troubleshooting to minimize service disruptions for our users and clients.

## 2. Applies to
This operational guideline is strictly limited in scope and applies exclusively to the Engineering department within the Company. Personnel outside this specific division are exempt from the requirements outlined herein. Team members belonging to other corporate functions are not expected to participate in these technical rotations or manage the associated production alerts.

## 3. Department rule
Engineers participate in a weekly on-call rotation for production incidents outside working hours. During their assigned weekly shift, designated staff members must remain reachable and prepared to diagnose unexpected system failures, performance degradation, or infrastructure outages that occur during nights, weekends, or holidays. Furthermore, the on-call engineer must acknowledge a critical alert within 30 minutes. This mandatory response time guarantees that high-priority notifications receive immediate attention, preventing minor issues from escalating into major operational crises.

## 4. Rationale
Implementing a structured rotation ensures that the burden of after-hours support is distributed fairly among all qualified technical staff at the Company, preventing individual burnout. By setting explicit timeframes for alert acknowledgment, leadership fosters a culture of accountability and responsiveness. Quick intervention protects revenue streams, maintains customer trust, and upholds service level agreements. Edge cases, such as overlapping holidays or planned time off during a rotation week, require proactive coverage swaps arranged well in advance with peer colleagues.

## 5. Escalation
When an issue proves too complex for the primary responder or requires specialized domain knowledge, a clear escalation path must be followed. If initial troubleshooting steps fail to resolve the underlying problem within a reasonable timeframe, or if the severity increases, the on-call staff member is responsible for notifying secondary technical leads or management. This hierarchical approach ensures that critical incidents receive multidisciplinary attention promptly, mitigating prolonged downtime and safeguarding the technological assets of the Company.
